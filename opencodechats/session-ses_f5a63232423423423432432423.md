## Supported Models[¶](#supported-models "Permanent link")

-   Any HuggingFace model can be converted using `cactus convert [HF-Name]`, though experimental.
-   Liquid, Gemma. whisper. parakeet and Qwen model families are especially tested.
-   Some models have been pre-uploaded [here](https://huggingface.co/Cactus-Compute), just run `cactus download [HF-Name]`.
-   `cactus run [HF-Name]` albeit first downloads or convert the model if not found.

# Cactus Hybrid Inference[¶](#cactus-hybrid-inference "Permanent link")

Even a well-quantized local model trails a frontier cloud model on the hardest queries. Cactus Hybrid Inference closes the gap: serve most queries locally, and route only the ones the local model is likely to get wrong to the cloud.

A lightweight probe (~65k parameters) attached to the local model's hidden states emits a single confidence score per generation. Queries above a threshold are sent to the cloud. The result: frontier-class accuracy at a fraction of the cloud cost, with most data never leaving the device.

## Why Hybrid?[¶](#why-hybrid "Permanent link")

A 2B-parameter model running locally handles the majority of user queries well. But there's a long tail of hard questions — complex reasoning, domain-specific knowledge, ambiguous multimodal inputs — where a frontier cloud model (Gemini Pro, Claude, GPT-4) would get it right and the local model won't.

The naive solutions don't work:

-   **Always local**: misses the hard queries. Users notice.
-   **Always cloud**: expensive, slow, and sends all data off-device.
-   **Token entropy as confidence**: anti-predictive on transcription and vision MCQ. High entropy often means the model is considering multiple valid candidates, not that it's wrong.

The probe solves this by reading the model's internal state directly — a much richer signal than output token probabilities.

## What the Probe Does[¶](#what-the-probe-does "Permanent link")

The probe reads the local model's hidden states after generation and outputs a single score: the probability the model got this query wrong. Above a threshold → route to cloud. Below → serve locally.

The probe:

-   **Adds negligible latency** — 65k parameters, runs after generation is already complete
-   **Handles all modalities** — text, vision, and audio through the same architecture, since it reads post-fusion hidden states where modalities are already merged
-   **Transfers to audio with zero audio training** — trained on text + vision only, yet achieves 0.79–0.88 AUROC on audio benchmarks
-   **Works across quantization levels** — same probe checkpoint at FP16, 4-bit, 3-bit; no per-quantization retraining needed

## Where It's Weakest[¶](#where-its-weakest "Permanent link")

1.  **When the local model is broken.** On MMLU-Pro at 4-bit, local accuracy drops to 0.28 and probe AUROC falls toward 0.5. Probe utility scales with local-model competence — when the model is broken, there's no correctness signal to read.

## The queue by exploration, not by timer

**The old idea (bad):** every 45s, enqueue a turn → the model re-describes an already-settled area → wasteful hallucination pressure on settled ground.

**The frontier model (调理 what you described):**

```
   settled chunks (fully populated — the GM stops touching them)
   ┌─────────────┐
   │  · populated│          @ = player
   ├──┬──────▓▓▓─┤   ▓▓▓ = the FRONTIER ring: unexplored chunks bordering
   │  │   ▓▓ @ ▓▓│         explored ones — where enemies live and
   └──┴──────────┘           where GM turns are EXPENSIVE
                          unexplored = dark, generated, not yet narrated
```

1. **Explored = a set of chunk keys** (the plane is already 32×32 chunks — free granularity). On the **first visit** to a fresh chunk the world emits a `frontier` gmEvent (`"Rae pushed into new country (chunk 2,-1)"`) — that's the trigger, no timer needed.
2. **Population degree (adjustable knob, `gm.harness frontier=true` style config):** a fresh chunk asks the GM for content **once** — after the GM has authored/existentially touched K entities in it (enemy groups, placements, narration), the chunk is **settled** and stops generating entities (your "populated to a certain degree" rule; exceptions slot into the same list — merchant ≠ loot ≠ enemy).
3. **Enemies stay on the border**: spawn tool calls get a **near-anchor mediation** from the rails — `near: "frontier"` resolves server-side to a walkable tile in the **ring of unexplored chunks touching settled ones**, not the middle of known country. The player never sees new wolves pop in-mid-settled-land — they meet them at the dark edge of the map, exactly as you framed.
